Will the Option Premium
Rise or Fall?

A practical framework for estimating whether a NIFTY 50 or SENSEX Call/Put premium is likely to increase or decrease — using price action, option Greeks, implied volatility, open interest, volume and time decay.

The first principle: premium is not the index

CALL Premium

Generally benefits when the underlying index rises. It can still fall despite a rising index if time decay and/or IV contraction outweigh the benefit from the index move.

PUT Premium

Generally benefits when the underlying index falls. It can still fall during a small index decline if theta and IV changes work against the position.

Key idea

Direction alone is insufficient. Premium is driven by the interaction of spot price, strike, expiry, volatility and interest rates.

The 5-factor premium prediction framework

① Direction

Is NIFTY/SENSEX trending up, down or sideways? Use higher highs/lows, VWAP, moving averages, market structure and important levels.

② Delta

Delta estimates how much the option premium changes for a 1-point change in the underlying, all else equal. Higher absolute delta means stronger directional sensitivity.

③ IV

Implied volatility represents the market's expected variability. A volatility increase can lift both Calls and Puts; a volatility collapse can hurt both.

④ Theta

Every passing day generally reduces extrinsic value. Near expiry, theta can become extremely aggressive, especially for near-the-money options.

⑤ OI + Volume

Open interest and volume help identify where market participants are concentrated. Combine them with price action rather than treating OI changes as a standalone signal.

Bonus: Gamma

Gamma measures how quickly delta changes. Near-the-money options close to expiry can have high gamma, making premium changes much faster when the index moves.

How to read Option Chain + OI

Potential bullish clues

  • Index holds support and forms higher highs/higher lows.
  • Call-side resistance is absorbed/broken with strong price action.
  • Put writing/support appears at relevant strikes.
  • Futures/spot momentum confirms the move.
  • Call IV and premium expand together during an upside move.

Potential bearish clues

  • Index rejects resistance and forms lower highs.
  • Important support breaks with volume.
  • Put-side support weakens or Put unwinding occurs around key strikes.
  • Futures/spot momentum confirms downside.
  • Put premium expands while downside momentum and/or IV support the move.

Important: “Call writing = bearish” or “Put writing = bullish” is a useful shorthand, not a guaranteed rule. OI can be created for hedging, spreads and other multi-leg strategies.

Premium prediction using Greeks

A useful approximation for a small move is:

Change in option premium ≈ Δ × Change in underlying + Vega × Change in IV − Theta × Time passed + Gamma effect

This is an approximation, not an exact pricing formula. Gamma becomes increasingly important when the underlying moves significantly or the option is close to expiry.

Worked example: NIFTY Call

Suppose a NIFTY Call has:

Premium ₹120Delta 0.50Theta ₹4/dayVega ₹2 per 1 IV point

If NIFTY rises 100 points, IV rises 1 point and one day passes:

Approximate change ≈ (0.50 × 100) + (2 × 1) − 4 = ₹48

So the premium could move roughly from ₹120 toward ₹168, before considering the full nonlinear gamma effect and other pricing inputs.

This is an educational illustration, not a prediction of an actual market premium. Real option prices depend on the live option chain and continuously changing variables.

A practical 15-minute pre-trade routine

  1. Mark previous day high/low, major support/resistance and current VWAP.
  2. Check NIFTY/SENSEX spot and futures structure.
  3. Review the option chain around ATM and nearby strikes.
  4. Compare Call/Put OI, OI change, volume and premium movement.
  5. Check ATM IV and whether volatility is expanding or contracting.
  6. Estimate Delta, Theta, Vega and Gamma for the selected strike.
  7. Estimate the index move required to reach your target premium.
  8. Ask: “What happens if the index moves sideways for the next hour/day?”
  9. Define stop-loss based on premium, underlying level, or strategy risk before entering.

Common mistakes to avoid

❌ “Index up = Call must rise”

Not necessarily. A small move can be overwhelmed by theta or IV contraction.

❌ Buying cheap OTM options

Low premium does not mean low risk. OTM options can lose most or all of their extrinsic value.

❌ Ignoring IV

Buying options immediately before a volatility event can expose you to IV crush afterward.

❌ Treating OI as certainty

OI does not reveal the complete strategy of market participants. Always combine it with price and volume.

The core formula to remember

Premium Direction = f(Spot Move, IV, Time, Delta, Gamma, Vega, Theta, Market Structure)

The best probability assessment comes from combining multiple independent signals rather than relying on one indicator.
