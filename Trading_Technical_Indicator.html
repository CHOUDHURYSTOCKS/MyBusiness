Technical Indicators Explained

Technical indicators help traders analyze market trends, momentum, price direction, and trading activity. This guide explains six commonly used indicators: Volume, SMA (20), EMA (20), WMA (20), Momentum (10), and ROC (10).

Key Technical Indicators

1. Volume

Volume represents the total number of shares, contracts, or units traded during a specific period. It measures market participation and trader interest.

Volume = Total Quantity Traded

Why It Matters

  • Confirms price movements.
  • Indicates market strength.
  • Helps identify breakouts and reversals.

Interpretation

  • High Volume + Rising Price = Strong bullish trend.
  • High Volume + Falling Price = Strong bearish trend.
  • Low volume often signals weak conviction.

2. SMA (20)

The Simple Moving Average (SMA) calculates the average closing price over the last 20 periods. It smooths out short-term fluctuations and helps identify the overall trend.

SMA(20) = Sum of Last 20 Closing Prices ÷ 20

Uses

  • Trend identification.
  • Support and resistance levels.
  • Moving average crossover strategies.

Interpretation

  • Price above SMA = Bullish sentiment.
  • Price below SMA = Bearish sentiment.
  • Flat SMA = Sideways market.

3. EMA (20)

The Exponential Moving Average gives greater weight to recent prices, making it more responsive than SMA.

EMA = (Price × Multiplier) + Previous EMA × (1 − Multiplier)

Multiplier = 2 ÷ (20 + 1)

Advantages

  • Faster reaction to price changes.
  • Better for short-term trading.
  • Useful in trending markets.

Interpretation

  • Price crossing above EMA may indicate buying strength.
  • Price crossing below EMA may indicate selling pressure.

4. WMA (20)

Weighted Moving Average assigns larger weights to recent prices and smaller weights to older prices.

WMA = Σ(Price × Weight) ÷ ΣWeights

Benefits

  • More responsive than SMA.
  • Highlights recent market activity.
  • Useful for identifying trend shifts.

Interpretation

  • Rising WMA suggests strengthening momentum.
  • Falling WMA indicates weakening price action.
  • Often preferred by active traders.

5. Momentum (10)

Momentum measures the rate of change in price over the last 10 periods. It indicates how quickly prices are moving.

Momentum(10) = Current Close − Close 10 Periods Ago

Purpose

  • Measures trend strength.
  • Identifies acceleration or deceleration.
  • Detects possible reversals.

Interpretation

  • Positive value = Bullish momentum.
  • Negative value = Bearish momentum.
  • Increasing values indicate strengthening trends.

6. ROC (10)

Rate of Change (ROC) measures percentage price change over the previous 10 periods. It is a momentum oscillator that highlights speed and direction of price movement.

ROC(10) = [(Current Price − Price 10 Periods Ago) ÷ Price 10 Periods Ago] × 100

Why Traders Use ROC

  • Measures percentage momentum.
  • Identifies overbought and oversold conditions.
  • Detects trend reversals.

Interpretation

  • ROC above 0 = Upward momentum.
  • ROC below 0 = Downward momentum.
  • Large positive values indicate strong bullish strength.

Quick Comparison

Volume measures participation, SMA provides smooth trend analysis, EMA reacts faster to price changes, WMA emphasizes recent prices, Momentum measures absolute price change, and ROC measures percentage price change. Combining these indicators often provides a more complete understanding of market conditions.
